Which seat are you in?

A cipher is not secure or insecure on its own. It is secure against a particular attacker, with particular powers. Change who is sitting where and the same cipher goes from fine to fatal, without one line of the cipher changing.

Two words that get mixed up

Threat model

The seating plan

Who holds the key. Who picks the plaintext. Who gets to see the ciphertext. How many times you may ask. It describes a situation, not a technique, and you write it down before you attack anything.

Attack

The move you make

A procedure that pulls out something you should not be able to get. It only means anything relative to a model. "AES is broken" says nothing. "ECB leaks which blocks are equal, to anyone who can see ciphertexts" is a claim about a seat.

Most of the work in breaking something is figuring out which seat you are in. The attack is often short once you know. Getting the seat wrong means you go looking for a clever trick when the answer was that you were allowed to ask the question.

Press "encrypt again" a few times. The ECB tiles never move, because ECB is a plain lookup from block to block with no randomness anywhere. The CBC tiles change every time, because each run picks a fresh random starting value. That difference is why one of them lets you compare two messages and the other does not.

Where this shows up

The chosen-plaintext seat needs two things, not one. You have to choose part of the input, and you have to see the ciphertext that comes out. Plenty of real systems hand you one half. The interesting question is always where the other half comes from.
